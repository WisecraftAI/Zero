# Portfolio Review Rubric

For reviewing junior / mid portfolios. Score each dimension 1-5; the pattern of scores matters more than the total.

## Rating scale

- 1 - Gap. Missing or at odds with the craft.
- 2 - Emerging. Awareness but inconsistent execution.
- 3 - Competent. Reliable at this level of complexity.
- 4 - Strong. Exceeds expectations; handles complexity.
- 5 - Exceptional. Teaches me something.

## 1. Problem framing (1-5)

Does each project clearly articulate the user problem, business context, and constraints before jumping to solutions?

- Red flags: "The company wanted a redesign." "I was assigned this project."
- Green flags: "Users with vision impairments couldn't complete onboarding; 18% support ticket share."

## 2. Research and evidence (1-5)

Is there evidence of understanding users beyond personal assumptions? Quant, qual, competitive, behavioral data?

- Red flags: "User research" as a word with no artifacts. Personas that read as demographics.
- Green flags: Quotes from interviews. Analytics snippets. Clearly described methods and sample.

## 3. Process and iteration (1-5)

Do we see how the work evolved? Multiple explorations? Abandoned directions and why?

- Red flags: Only polished final screens.
- Green flags: Sketches, wireframes, abandoned options with notes on why, iteration driven by feedback.

## 4. Interaction and IA (1-5)

Are flows clear, efficient, forgiving? Does the IA match user mental models?

- Red flags: Screens only, no flow diagrams. Ambiguous navigation. Irreversible actions without safety.
- Green flags: Flow diagrams. Consideration of error paths, empty states, edge cases.

## 5. Visual craft (1-5)

Typography, hierarchy, spacing, color, consistency. Do the visuals read cleanly at a glance?

- Red flags: Multiple styles in one project. Inconsistent spacing. Weak hierarchy.
- Green flags: Intentional rhythm. Clear primary actions. Polish at all zoom levels.

## 6. Systems thinking (1-5)

Do they think in tokens, components, patterns? Or one-off screens?

- Red flags: Every screen visibly bespoke. No component library referenced.
- Green flags: Explicit use of a design system (theirs or the company's). Discussion of reusable patterns.

## 7. Accessibility (1-5)

Evidence of inclusive design consideration.

- Red flags: No mention. Low-contrast choices. No alt text implied.
- Green flags: Contrast called out. Keyboard flows considered. WCAG awareness.

## 8. Outcomes (1-5)

Did the work ship? What happened? Learning from live results?

- Red flags: No outcomes, no metrics.
- Green flags: Metrics moved, or a clear story of what was learned, including if results were mixed.

## 9. Communication (1-5)

Can they tell the story? Is the portfolio itself well-designed and written?

- Red flags: Walls of text. Missing context. Overclaimed impact.
- Green flags: Structured narrative. Clear attribution of their contribution vs. team's.

## 10. Self-awareness (1-5)

Do they discuss trade-offs, limitations, what they'd do differently?

- Red flags: Everything was a success. Every decision was theirs.
- Green flags: Honest about tension, constraints, failed experiments, team contributions.

---

## Level calibration

**Junior candidate profile (target average ~2.5-3.3):**
- Problem framing: 3
- Research: 2-3
- Process: 3
- Interaction/IA: 2-3
- Visual craft: 3
- Systems: 2
- Accessibility: 2
- Outcomes: 2
- Communication: 3
- Self-awareness: 2-3

**Mid candidate (~3.3-4.0):**
- All dimensions 3+, at least 2-3 at 4.

**Senior (~4.0+):**
- Most at 4, several at 5. Multiple domains; clear strategic thinking; visible mentorship or system contribution.

---

## Interview follow-ups

Good portfolio reviews lead to conversations. For each project, have ready:

- "Walk me through a decision you regret."
- "What did you try first that didn't work?"
- "What did you have to fight for?"
- "What did the team disagree about?"
- "What would you measure differently next time?"
- "What would you hand off to the next designer?"

These reveal judgment far better than the final renders.
