# Usability Test Moderator Script

Adapt per study. Italicized text is moderator stage-direction, not to be read aloud.

---

## Intro (3-4 min)

"Hi [participant name], thanks so much for joining today. I'm [name] and I'll be walking you through this session. Before we get started, a few things.

First, we're testing a product, not you. There are no right or wrong answers - we want your honest reactions. If something feels off or confusing, that's really helpful for us to hear.

I didn't design this, so you won't hurt my feelings with any feedback - the more direct the better.

I'd like to record this session so I don't have to take notes at the same time. Only my team and I will see it. Is that OK?

*(wait for consent)*

Also, as you go through, could you think out loud? Whatever's in your head - even if it feels silly or unrelated. It helps us understand what you're seeing."

## Warm-up (3-5 min)

"To get started, could you tell me a bit about yourself - what you do, and how you usually [relevant behavior, e.g. 'manage your finances' / 'order food online' / 'watch movies at home']?"

*Follow up on details. Build rapport. Get them talking and comfortable with thinking aloud.*

## Context (5 min)

Choose 2-3 context questions relevant to the task:

- "When was the last time you [did relevant task]?"
- "Walk me through what you did - where were you, what were you trying to do, what tools did you use?"
- "What was frustrating or slow about that?"

*Listen for their current mental model and vocabulary. Use their words later.*

## Task 1: [Name]

"Okay, I'm going to share a screen with you. [Or: I'm going to hand you this phone.] I'd like you to imagine a scenario.

**Scenario:** [e.g. 'You just got paid. You owe your roommate $50 for utilities. You want to send it to them.']

Take a look and see what you'd do. Remember to talk out loud."

*Stay silent. Don't prompt. If they pause for 5+ seconds, you can say: "What are you thinking?"*

*If they ask for help, deflect: "What do you think you'd do if I weren't here?"*

*Do NOT explain the design. Do NOT point out features.*

**Post-task probes:**

- "How was that?"
- "Was anything surprising or confusing?"
- "How confident are you that you completed the task correctly?" (1-7)
- "Was that easier or harder than you expected?"

## Wrap-up (5 min)

"A few final questions."

- "Overall, what's your impression of this [product / feature]?"
- "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
- "How does this compare to [current tool / competitor] if you use one?"
- "Would you use this for [relevant job]? Why or why not?"

*Optional standardized measures:*

- **SUS** (System Usability Scale) - 10 agree/disagree items, produces 0-100 score. Benchmarkable.
- **UMUX-Lite** - 2 items, short version of SUS-like measure.
- **Single Ease Question (SEQ)** - "Overall, how easy or difficult was it to complete these tasks?" 1-7.
- **Net Promoter Score** - "How likely are you to recommend this to a friend or colleague?" 0-10.

## Close

"That's all the questions I have. Is there anything we didn't cover that you'd like to share, or any questions for me?

*(listen)*

Thanks so much - this has been really valuable. Your incentive should arrive via [method] within [timeframe]. Have a great rest of your day."

*End recording.*

---

## Post-session checklist (moderator, within 1 hour)

- [ ] Stop and save recording
- [ ] Jot top 3 observations while fresh
- [ ] Note any follow-up questions to ask in future sessions
- [ ] Deliver incentive if not automated
- [ ] Update notes in shared tracker

## Moderator notes

**Watch yourself for:**
- Leading ("Was that easy?" vs "How was that?")
- Filling silence (count to 5 after answers)
- Explaining the design (never)
- Defending ("well, we did it that way because...")
- Nodding encouragement (neutral face)
- Hint-dropping ("you could try...")

**When they struggle, struggle with them.** Don't rescue. The struggle is the data.
